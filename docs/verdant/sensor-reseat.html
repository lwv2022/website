Reseat the CO2 sensor wires

Six rooms have a CO2/temp/humidity sensor that the controller cannot hear. The likely cause is inside the black inline coupler on the sensor cable: the wire insulation is sitting in the screw clamp, so the screw is gripping plastic instead of copper.

The whole job, in one line: open the coupler, pull each wire out, strip a little more insulation, push the bare copper all the way in, tighten, tug, close it back up. About 10 minutes per room.

What you are looking for

This is the coupler. Each end unscrews and holds four screw terminals. The photos below are from the product listing, so the labels on the terminals (L1, L2, N, ground) are for house wiring. Ignore those labels. We only care that each wire is clamped on bare copper.

Bad clamp vs good clamp

Tools

  • Wire strippers (or a sharp knife, carefully). The wires are thin, so go gently.
  • Small flat screwdriver, about 2.5 mm tip, for the terminal screws.
  • Your phone on the Verdant Wi-Fi, to confirm the fix (see the last section).

Steps for each room

  1. Find the coupler on the sensor cable

    Follow the cable from the white SenseCAP sensor. The coupler is the black barrel with a yellow band. You do not need to power anything down; the sensor runs on low-voltage DC.

  2. Open one end at a time

    Unscrew the locking nut on the sensor side and slide it back along the cable. Unscrew the shell and slide it back too. The terminal insert is now exposed with four wires screwed into it. Do the sensor side first, then repeat everything for the other side.

  3. Check every wire before you touch it

    Look at where each wire enters its terminal. If you can see coloured insulation disappearing into the clamp, that wire is the problem. Give each wire a gentle pull. Anything that slides out, or that was only holding by its sleeve, needs redoing. Even if only one wire looks bad, redo all four so you know they are right.

  4. Loosen the screw and pull the wire out

    Back the screw out a few turns until the wire comes free. Do not remove the screw completely; it is easy to lose.

  5. Strip to about 8 mm of bare copper

    Strip the insulation back so that 8 to 10 mm of copper is showing (about 3/8 of an inch, or the depth of the terminal). Twist the strands tightly together so nothing sticks out sideways. If the end of the copper is broken or corroded, cut it off clean and strip fresh.

  6. Push it all the way in, then tighten

    Insert the bare copper into the same terminal it came out of, until the insulation stops against the terminal body. Hold it there and tighten the screw firmly. You should not see any copper outside the terminal, and no insulation inside it.

  7. Tug test

    Pull on each wire with a firm tug. Nothing should move. If a wire pulls out, it was gripping strands only; re-twist, re-insert, re-tighten.

  8. Check the two unused wires

    The sensor cable has six wires but only four are used. The brown and orange wires must not be in any terminal and must not touch anything. Trim them short and make sure they are tucked back inside the cable jacket or taped off individually.

  9. Close it up and do the other end

    Slide the shell back on and screw it down. Make sure the green rubber ring is inside the locking nut, then tighten the nut so it grips the cable. Now repeat steps 2 to 9 on the other side of the coupler. Wires on that side may be different colours; that is fine. What matters is that each terminal position has a wire on both halves, clamped on copper.

Which wire is which

These are the sensor's own cable colours, straight from the SenseCAP manual. Use them to sanity-check the sensor side. You do not need to rewire anything, just make sure nothing got moved between terminals while you were working.

  • Yellow RS-485 A (data +)
  • Green RS-485 B (data −)
  • Red Power + (DC supply)
  • Black Power − (ground)
  • Brown Not used. Must not be connected.
  • Orange Not used. Must not be connected.
STOP
If A and B end up swapped, the sensor stays silent. If red and black are swapped, the sensor can be damaged. If you are unsure which wire was in which terminal, take a photo before you pull anything and send it to us.

Confirm it worked

You can check from your phone in the room, without going to a computer.

  1. Connect your phone to the Verdant site Wi-Fi.
  2. Open http://192.168.11.1:8080 in the browser. This is the site's GCU directory page.
  3. Find the controller you just worked on (for example GCU7D78 for room A2).
  4. Give it up to 5 minutes. The pod's line should change from red online (2/3) to green online (3/3). That third device is the sensor.
  5. Open the pod's own page from the directory. Temperature, humidity and CO2 should show real numbers instead of 0. CO2 takes about 2 minutes to warm up after the sensor gets power back.
OK
Green 3/3 and a real CO2 number means that room is done. Tick it off in the table above and move to the next one.

If it still shows 2/3

  • Re-open both ends of the coupler and tug every wire again. One loose wire is enough.
  • Open the sensor's cover. There is a small LED on the board that lights while the sensor powers up and warms up. If nothing on the board lights when you reconnect the coupler, no power is reaching it: check the red and black wires first.
  • Look for a second coupler or splice further along the cable toward the controller and give it the same treatment.
  • Check the cable itself for pinches, cuts or a crushed section where it passes through a wall or tray.
  • If everything looks right and it is still 2/3, the sensor itself may have failed. Take a photo of both terminal inserts and the sensor board and send them to us; we will swap the sensor.
