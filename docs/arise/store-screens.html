How the rooms are picked

Nobody has to choose cameras by hand. Each morning the page looks at every room's new photo and keeps the ones that sell it: a crop in week 6 of flower or later, today's photo taken with the lights on, a clear picture, and a full canopy. Rooms Mitch wants on the screen stay pinned at the top. As a room is harvested it drops off and the next one takes its place.

What shoppers see

  • Four screens that rotate on their own: the best room today, this week's four, what is coming soon, and one strain at three ages, from flip to harvest.
  • Each picture carries the strain, the flower day and how long until harvest, straight from the farm's grow records.
  • The pictures update themselves as the new daily photos arrive. No one at the store touches it.
  • The screens use Arise's own look: the logo, the flame orange and teal, and the typeface from arisecannabis.com. The store's line runs along the bottom, and a small Legacy West Ventures mark sits in its own corner.

What it never shows

  • Room temperature, humidity or CO2. Those stay inside the farm's tools.
  • The camera's date stamp and room number. They are cropped off every picture.
  • Empty rooms, dark rooms, or rooms with no crop on record.
  • Anything live. A photo taken with the lights on always looks better than a night-time stream.

What it takes at the store

Screens
The two end TVs, in landscape as they are now. The three menu boards in the middle stay as they are.
Computer
The mini PC already driving the end TVs. The page is light, so it stops the lag the four live streams cause today, and the two TVs no longer need to mirror each other.
Setup
One web link per TV, opened once in Chrome's kiosk mode. That also removes the browser toolbar and taskbar that show on the screens today.
Day to day
Nothing. If the internet drops, the last pictures stay up until it is back.
